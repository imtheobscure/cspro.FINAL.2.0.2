# 21 · Fast Words ⚡

**Band:** Young (6–10) · **Type:** Game (screen, fluency sprint) · **Time:** 5–6 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
One target word, and moles pop up carrying it and its lookalikes (was/saw, their/there,
when/then). The child hits only the target. Fluent reading is automatic word recognition — a
strong reader identifies a common word in under 200 ms — and a short timed drill pushes
recognition out of the deliberate system into the automatic one. The decoys are the errors
children genuinely make, so the practice lands where it matters. (Sight-word automaticity work.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **recognises the target word** among confusable decoys, fast.
- The child's **error pattern** on lookalikes becomes visible to the tutor.
- The child experiences a **short, intense sprint** — 40 seconds, never more.
- The tutor leaves with the one decoy pair to practise this week.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. "Hit only the word: went" — the target stays on screen the whole time.
3. Moles pop with the target and 3–5 lookalikes; hit the target, let the others pass.
4. A hit on a decoy costs nothing but a wobble — no life lost, no score lost.
5. 40 seconds on the clock (gentle ring; not a countdown of doom).
6. **After the sprint**: no scoreboard — just the two words that tricked them, read together.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- One target word per sprint; decoys must be truly confusable.
- 30–45 seconds is the whole game; a sprint, not a lesson.
- Private score only — the child's own best, never compared to others.
- Between sprints, re-teach the decoy pair on paper; the game is the check, not the teaching.
