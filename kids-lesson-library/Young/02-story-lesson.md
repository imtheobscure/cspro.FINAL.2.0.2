# 02 · Story Lesson

**Band:** Young (5–12) · **Type:** Lesson (story + comprehension) · **Time:** 30–40 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
A five-part story — a character who wants something, a small problem, a turn, a resolution — of
six to ten sentences, with the target words woven naturally inside it. Then two comprehension
questions the child answers in a full sentence, and one "what would you do?" question. Story
first is deliberate: a child who is following a character is doing the hardest comprehension
work without noticing. (Cambridge story-based primary method; Oxford Reading Tree model.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **follows a story in English** and retells its order (beginning/middle/end).
- The child **answers 2 comprehension questions in full sentences**.
- The child **uses 4–6 target words** that the story carried.
- The child **predicts and decides** ("what would you do?") — comprehension beyond the text.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **Before reading** — the character and their wish; one prediction question.
3. **The story** — one scene per screen (5 scenes), big picture, one narrator line each.
4. **Order it** — drag three scene cards back into order.
5. **Two questions** — full-sentence answers modelled first, then the child's turn.
6. **What would you do?** — the child chooses an answer; both are valid; explain why.
7. **Wrap** — retell in three sentences + one sentence they are proud of + family note.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- Model every full sentence before asking for one ("I think the fox is hungry. What do you think?").
- Never rush the prediction — the thinking is the lesson.
- If the child retells in one word, expand it aloud and let them repeat the full sentence.
- Keep each scene to 2–4 sentences; pictures carry the rest.
