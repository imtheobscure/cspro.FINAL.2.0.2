# 04 · Role-play & Drama

**Band:** Young (6–12) · **Type:** Lesson (speaking / drama) · **Time:** 25–35 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
A tiny scene the child acts out — a shop, a café, a doctor, a lost puppy — with two roles and
three fixed sentence frames per role the child can lean on. One twist lets the child choose what
happens next. Drama gives a child a *reason* to speak: they are not answering a question, they
are being someone with a problem. (Maley & Duff drama method; role-play in every communicative
primary course.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **performs 4–6 speaking turns** using the frames.
- The child **carries a whole scene** without switching to L1.
- The child **improvises one line** at the twist — the first step past the script.
- The tutor has the exact frames, role cards and praise lines written out.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **Set-up** — the place, the two roles, the props to grab (a bag, a cup, a toy dog).
3. **Frames** — three sentences per role; practise each with the model voice first.
4. **Scene 1** — run the whole scene with the frames; the child reads/taps their lines.
5. **Twist** — two choices for what happens next; both are easy to act.
6. **Scene 2** — re-run with the twist; no frames shown (optional support button).
7. **Wrap** — "one line you said without looking" + family note to perform it at home.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- Take the harder role yourself so the child's lines stay under eight words.
- Never correct mid-scene; note errors and recast them after the bow.
- The bow matters: clap, "thank you, actors!" — drama ends with applause.
- Frame lines are support, not scripts; encourage a word changed.
