# 08 · Quiz Show Lesson

**Band:** Young (5–12) · **Type:** Lesson (retrieval practice) · **Time:** 20–30 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Six to ten questions on this lesson's words plus two reviewing last lesson, in the shape of a
game show: big questions, four answers, stars for the class. Retrieval practice — pulling an
answer out of memory — beats re-reading by a wide margin, and a quiz is the cheapest way to make
every child in the room retrieve. The two review questions at the start are spaced retrieval in
disguise. (Rosenshine's retrieval practice; classroom quiz-show format.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **retrieves 6+ items** from memory (not recognition of a list).
- The child **reviews last lesson's language** inside the quiz opener.
- The child **explains one answer** ("why is that one right?").
- The tutor sees the **class gaps** on a private summary at the end.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **Round 1–2 · Review** — two questions from last lesson; easy on purpose.
3. **Rounds 3–8 · Today** — 4 options each, rotated answer position, no visual giveaway.
4. **Say why** — after two questions, ask "why?"; a modelled reason appears; the child repeats it.
5. **Team answers** — the child can phone a friend (ask the tutor), once per game.
6. **Scoreboard** — stars for the class; the tutor's private tally holds the points.
7. **Wrap** — the two hardest items replayed; "you remembered X without looking!" + family note.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- Rotate who answers; never the fastest hand only.
- Options within two words of each other in length; longest option never correct.
- If the child is guessing, drop one option — three is kinder for a struggling reader.
- Keep the tally private; the class sees stars, never a loser.
