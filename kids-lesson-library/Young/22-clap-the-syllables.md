# 22 · Clap the Syllables 👏

**Band:** Young (5–8) · **Type:** Game (screen, phonological awareness) · **Time:** 5–7 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
The child hears a word, claps the beats, then presses how many. Breaking a word into beats is
phonological awareness in its most concrete form — at this age it predicts decoding better than
knowing letters does — and it is done with the body, so a child who cannot yet read a word can
still succeed. The count is pressed, not said, so a quiet child answers as loudly as a loud one.
(Phonological awareness practice; Letters & Sounds Phase 1.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **claps and counts 5–6 words** of one to four syllables.
- The child **feels the beats** before seeing any spelling.
- The child **writes the hyphens** for two words (5+ readers) — beats made visible.
- The tutor sees which syllable patterns are still shaky.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. A word card appears with a big picture; the word is spoken slowly.
3. **Clap together** — one clap per beat, animated hands help the pace.
4. The child presses 1–4; correct = the syllable bubbles pop in time.
5. Every word shows its hyphens after the answer ("but-ter-fly") — never before.
6. Finish: the child's own name clapped, then a family member's + one for home.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- Words 1–4 syllables, shortest first, lesson words before random ones.
- Always clap with the child; the count is felt, not calculated.
- Names are the best material — the child's, the pet's, the tutor's.
- Pressing the number is the answer; speaking it is a bonus, never required.
