# 17 · Sentence Builder 🧱

**Band:** Young (7–12) · **Type:** Game (screen, word order) · **Time:** 6–9 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Word tiles shuffle on screen; the child taps them into the right order and the sentence
assembles. Word order is where meaning lives in English, and moving tiles makes the pattern
visible in a way a worksheet cannot: the child watches the sentence build and hears the wrong
version in their head. Doing it as a physical sequence also links word order to the rhythm of
the spoken sentence. (Sentence-order tasks in primary EFL; Montessori grammar strips.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **orders 3–4 sentences** of 4–7 words.
- The child **hears the sentence** read correctly after building it.
- The child **spots one distractor** word that does not belong.
- The tutor sees whether word order or vocabulary is the blocker.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. Tiles appear shuffled: all the sentence words + 1–2 plausible distractors.
3. The child taps tiles into the sentence slots; wrong placements can be pulled back.
4. When complete: the sentence is read aloud, punctuation included, with a happy chime.
5. **Read it** — the child reads the sentence from the screen (tutor helps as needed).
6. Next round: one sentence from today's language. Finish: all sentences read in a row.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- 4–7 words per sentence; include the punctuation on the last tile.
- At most two distractors, both plausible for the sentence.
- Vocabulary the child already met in the lesson — this game is about order, not new words.
- If it is too easy, hide the first word's tile until later.
