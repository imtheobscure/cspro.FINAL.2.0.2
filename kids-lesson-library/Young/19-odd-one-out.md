# 19 · Odd One Out 🤔

**Band:** Young (7–12) · **Type:** Game (screen, reasoning) · **Time:** 6–9 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Four words; three share something, one does not. The child finds the odd one **and says why** —
that reason is the whole game. Noticing why a word does not fit is reasoning, not vocabulary:
the child tests each item against a rule they hold in their head. A correct tap with no
explanation is a guess; an explanation is understanding. (Reasoning tasks from primary thinking
skills programmes.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **solves 4 puzzles** and gives a one-sentence reason for each.
- The child **uses category words aloud** (animal, feeling, action, long, short).
- The tutor hears whether the child can **articulate a rule** — the transferable skill.
- The child accepts that a different-but-defensible answer can be right too.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. Four giant word tiles appear.
3. The child taps the odd one; if right, the **reason** is asked for out loud before any reveal.
4. The model reason appears; the child repeats it in their own words.
5. If the child picks a different one **with a good reason**, the game accepts it ("you found
   another rule — excellent").
6. Finish: 4 solved + "You found the rules!" + one puzzle to try at home.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- Exactly one odd per puzzle, and the reason must be sayable in one short sentence.
- No trick rounds with two equally valid answers — except the deliberate optional bonus.
- Reward the explanation more than the tap.
- If the child can't say why, give the two candidate rules and let them choose.
