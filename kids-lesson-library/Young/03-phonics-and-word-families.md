# 03 · Phonics & Word Families

**Band:** Young (5–8) · **Type:** Lesson (phonics / decoding) · **Time:** 20–30 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
One word family (‑at, ‑ig, ‑op) taught by blending: c‑a‑t → cat, then h‑a‑t, m‑a‑t, plus two
nonsense words to prove the child is decoding, not memorising. Ends with a three-word dictation.
Systematic phonics is the strongest predictor of early reading success; the nonsense words are
the honesty check every good phonics programme uses. (Synthetic phonics; DfE Letters & Sounds
sequence.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **blends 4–6 real words** of one family out loud.
- The child **decodes 2 nonsense words** (zop, hig) — the real test of the rule.
- The child **writes 3 words** from dictation, with the tutor's model.
- The child hears the family's sound **in 10+ words** across the lesson.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **Hear it** — the sound of the day, in three words the child already knows.
3. **Build it** — tap letter tiles in order; the word assembles and is spoken.
4. **Family lap** — 4–6 real words, shortest first; blending animation each time.
5. **Trick check** — 2 nonsense words; if the child says them, the rule is real.
6. **Dictation** — hear a word, write it (paper or finger on screen), then see the model.
7. **Wrap** — read the three dictated words back + family note for 5 minutes at home.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- One family per lesson. Mixing families halves the effect.
- Always say the pure sound (/m/, not "muh") and blend smoothly.
- Model first, do it together, then the child alone — never skip the together beat.
- The nonsense words are not a trick on the child; celebrate them as the "super word test".
