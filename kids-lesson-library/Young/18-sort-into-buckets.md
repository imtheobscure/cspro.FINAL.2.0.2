# 18 · Sort Into Buckets 🗂️

**Band:** Young (6–12) · **Type:** Game (screen, categorising) · **Time:** 8–12 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Six to nine words drop into two or three buckets — past/present, noun/verb, long/short vowel,
things that go in water / don't. Sorting is how a child builds the categories that later do the
work of grammar: deciding *where* an item goes forces a comparison between items, a far deeper
act than naming them one by one. (Concept sorts from primary pedagogy; Montessori grammar
sorting.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **sorts 8 words** into the right buckets, saying each one aloud.
- The child **states the rule** for one bucket ("past means it already happened").
- Every bucket ends **non-empty** — the whole board is a complete idea.
- The tutor sees which rule is wobbly before the next lesson.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. Two or three giant buckets with one-word names + emoji; a tray of word tiles below.
3. Drag/tap a word; it flies to a bucket; correct = chime + the word is spoken.
4. Wrong = gentle wobble + "check it again" — the word stays in play.
5. **Say the rule** after the board completes: pick a bucket, explain it in one sentence.
6. Finish: the empty tray + "Every word in the right place!" + one replay with 3 new words.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- 2–3 buckets, each with a one-word name a child can say.
- Every item must be unambiguously in exactly one bucket; no trick items.
- Every bucket must end up non-empty (design the set so it does).
- Ask for the reason aloud — the explanation is the learning.
