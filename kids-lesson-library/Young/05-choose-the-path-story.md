# 05 · Choose-the-Path Story

**Band:** Young (6–12) · **Type:** Lesson (reading / prediction) · **Time:** 25–35 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
A branching story with three decision points, two child-friendly options each, and all paths
rejoining to a happy ending that reuses the target words. Prediction is the comprehension skill
that carries reading: a child guessing what comes next is building meaning, not decoding — and
because the story is *theirs*, they retell it at home without being asked. (Interactive fiction
in Khan Academy Kids style; "choose your own adventure" readers.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **makes 3 story decisions** and explains one ("why did you pick that?").
- The child **reads or listens to 8–12 sentences** and keeps the plot in mind.
- The child **retells their own path** in 3 sentences at the end.
- Target words appear in **every branch**, so no path loses the language.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **The setup** — a character with a wish; one sentence; the first choice appears.
3. **Decision points** — two options per screen, both safe, both leading to 3–4 sentence scenes.
4. **Consequence** — short scene; the choice is never punished ("the long way is the fun way").
5. **All paths rejoin** — the last scene is shared, so the class ends together.
6. **Why?** — the child explains one choice; modelled answer shown.
7. **Wrap** — retell your path in three sentences + family note.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- Every option must be reasonable — a child must never regret a choice.
- Read the two options aloud for younger readers; the choice is the point, not decoding.
- If the child asks to go back, let them; prediction improves with re-runs.
- Keep each branch scene to 3–4 sentences, under 12 words a sentence.
