# 05 · One-Word Play

**Band:** Very Young (2–5) · **Type:** Lesson (vocabulary, comprehension-first) · **Time:** 4–6 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
One screen, one enormous picture, one word. The adult says it, the child points at it, then
tries saying it; the game repeats with the same word three times before a second word appears.
Labeling is the highest-value language act at this age, and comprehension (pointing) is years
ahead of production (speaking) — so the win never depends on a word coming out. (Montessori
three-period lesson; Lingokids' "look, listen, say".)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **points to the right picture** for 2–3 words (comprehension, the real goal).
- The child **attempts the word** — any approximation counts, nothing is corrected.
- The child hears each word **5+ times** and sees it used in one tiny phrase.

## 3 · Interaction flow
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. **Look** — one giant picture, the word spoken twice, no text for the child to read.
3. **Show me** — "Where is the duck?" — the child points (only 2 pictures at this age).
4. **You try** — the child's turn: the microphone stays optional, pressing the star is enough.
5. **Same word, new place** — hidden among two other pictures; point again.
6. Finish: "You found the duck 3 times!" + the family line.

## 4 · Tutor notes
- One word per activity, maximum two syllables; two picture options, never three.
- Never say no; "nearly — look for the yellow one" is the correction.
- The child may only point for weeks; speak the word yourself for them.
- Repeat the same three words all week — repetition with tiny variation is the method.
