# 14 · Rhyme Time 🎵

**Band:** Very Young (3–5) · **Type:** Game (screen) · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Six pictures in a jumble; the child finds the two whose names end with the same sound
(cat–hat, duck–truck, star–car). To hear rhyme, a child must stop listening to what a word
*means* and start listening to how it *sounds* — exactly the shift reading demands. Rhyme is the
best preschool predictor of reading success, and it is entirely oral. (Nursery-rhyme practice;
phonemic-awareness research.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **matches 3 rhyming pairs** by sound, not spelling.
- The child **produces a rhyme attempt** ("cat… hat!") — attempts count.
- The tutor sees which ending sounds the child can already hear.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. Six giant pictures shuffle in; the tutor says each word twice (buttons per picture).
3. **Find the pair** — tap two; a true rhyme locks them with a chime; a miss wobbles both.
4. After each pair, the game says the two words together, stressing the ending.
5. Three pairs found → the pictures sing their endings one last time.
6. Replay with the same pairs (recommended), then optionally one new pair.

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- Rhymes must be **sound rhymes**, not spelling rhymes (love/move is a trap — avoid).
- Say the pair aloud before the child chooses; end the words slowly.
- If the child matches meaning (duck–pond), name both and say the endings — no correction.
- Three pairs is the whole game; more is overload at this age.
