# 17 · Sort It Out 🧺

**Band:** Very Young (3–5) · **Type:** Game (screen, sorting) · **Time:** 4–5 min · **Runtime:** one HTML file

## 1 · What it is
Four to six big pictures move into one of **two** baskets — food/not food, big/small, soft/hard,
things that go in water / things that don't. Categorisation is the beginning of thinking in
concepts: the same mental move that later sorts words into families and letters into sounds. Two
buckets is the right load at this age; a third costs the child the rule itself. (Montessori
sorting; preschool concept sorts.)

## 2 · Goals
- The child **sorts 5 items** into two named baskets, saying each word as it moves.
- The child hears the **category words** repeatedly (food, toy, soft, hard…).
- The tutor sees whether the child holds a two-way rule — a real cognitive milestone.

## 3 · How a round runs
1. Splash → "Let's go!".
2. Two giant baskets with one-word labels + emoji; a tray of items below.
3. Drag/tap an item; it flies to a basket; a chime or a gentle wobble answers.
4. Each item is **named aloud** as it moves (the game says the word).
5. Board completes → a tidy-up dance + "You sorted everything!".
6. One new board available: same categories, new items (repetition with variation).

## 4 · Tutor & room notes
- Exactly two categories, both with names a 3-year-old already knows.
- Every item must fit exactly one basket — no ambiguous items, ever.
- If the child sorts "wrong" but has a reason, ask about the reason; it is often a good one.
- Physical version: two bowls on the floor and a pile of real things.
