📄 Reading Passage — Urban Green Spaces
Cities around the world are rediscovering the power of parks. Urban green spaces do far more than decorate a skyline: they cool neighbourhoods during heatwaves, absorb storm water, and give residents a place to walk, cycle and meet neighbours. Studies in Singapore and Melbourne found that districts with dense tree cover can be up to five degrees cooler than nearby concrete-heavy areas. Access to nature has also been linked to lower stress and faster recovery after illness. For these reasons, city planners now treat green infrastructure as essential — not optional — when designing new districts.

📖 Read
🌟 Model Answer — Task 2 (band 9)
While housing and transport rightly compete for space in modern cities, I strongly disagree with the view that parks waste valuable land. Well-designed green spaces deliver benefits that no office block or road can replace. Firstly, they improve public health. Neighbourhoods with accessible parks report lower rates of stress-related illness, and children who play outdoors develop stronger immune systems. Secondly, parks are a city's natural air-conditioning: during summer heatwaves, tree cover measurably reduces temperatures and lowers energy demand. Finally, green space brings communities together — markets, concerts and weekend football turn strangers into neighbours. In short, parks are not an optional extra but the lungs of a healthy city, and removing them to build would cost more than any square metre of land is worth.
