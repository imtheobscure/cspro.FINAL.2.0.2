🔎 Section 1: What Is a Blockchain?

⏱ 15 mins · ⚡ 100 XP
📖 Read
📖 The Big Idea
Think of a blockchain as a shared notebook that thousands of computers hold at the same time. When someone writes a new page, every computer checks it and adds the same page to its own notebook. Because no single person owns the notebook, nobody can quietly change a page after it has been written. That is why people use blockchains to record money (Bitcoin), track goods in shops, and prove who owns what. Key idea: a blockchain is a shared record that nobody can secretly edit.

💭 Reflect: What Would You Use It For?
Think of three everyday records in your life (school marks, salary slips, medicine history). Which one would you trust on a blockchain, and why? Sample thinking: I would trust my medical history because no hospital could lose or edit it — but I would want to control who reads it. If blockchains are public, what could go wrong with putting personal data on one? Sample thinking: Everyone could see it forever, so private data needs permission or encryption. Do you think 'trust without a middleman' is always a good thing? Why or why not? Sample thinking: It removes fees and gatekeepers, but also removes someone to call when things go wrong.

✍️ Reflect & Write It Down
In 3-4 sentences, write what happens from the moment you send money until it appears on the chain. Model answer: I send the transaction, and nodes pass it around the network. Miners check it is valid and group it with others into a block. The block stores the previous block's hash, linking them like a chain. Finally every node adds the block to its own copy of the ledger. Which word was hardest to explain — hash or node? Write your own definition for it. Model answer: A node is any computer that keeps a full copy of the blockchain and helps check new records.
